    About these estimates

    Every number starts from official data. Rice area comes from the Office of Agricultural Economics (OAE). The emission factors, and the shares of irrigated and flood-prone rice, come from Thailand’s first Biennial Transparency Report (BTR1). Satellites are used only to decide where the methane goes within each province (the Open-SEA-Rice-10 rice map and Sentinel-2 dry-season water) and when in the year it is released (MODIS planting dates, with each crop emitting evenly over 120 days). 1 Gg = 1,000 tonnes.

    • Annual map: each year uses that year’s official rice area with the same emission factors and shares, so changes between years come from rice area, not from changes in how rice is farmed. From 2000 to 2022 our national total stays within 2% of the official BTR1 figure. Bueng Kan is counted with Nong Khai up to 2011 and on its own from 2012; to keep that change out of the averages, the ranking uses one year in 2012 and two years in 2013. Before 1994, Amnat Charoen, Nong Bua Lamphu and Sa Kaeo are counted with the provinces they were split from.
    • Monthly map: one typical year rather than a calendar year. Rice area is the 2022–24 average and planting dates run from May 2022 to May 2023. Each value is the tonnes of CH₄ in one 10 km (0.1°) cell. The cells show where the methane comes from, but timing is set for each province, so all cells in a province rise and fall together. 4,379 cells have rice methane: 3,839 with main-season rice and 540 with dry-season rice only.
    • Each map keeps one colour scale for every year or month, so a change in colour is a real change in the estimate. The scale is square-root, which lets small and large values show together; anything above the top of the scale gets the top colour.
